Challenges for minimal warm inflation in the Standard Model and beyond

Published: 2026-11-06 | Authors: Anish Ghoshal, Alessandro Strumia | Tags: hep-ph, astro-ph.CO

Abstract

A minimal realisation of warm inflation, even within the Standard Model, assumes that the inflaton $φ$ couples via the effective operator $φ\, G\tilde G$. We show that the SM solutions are in tension with the plasma calculation (as each sphaleron transition releases an energy much larger than the temperature) and with the assumed effective operator description. Embedding $φ$ as the Goldstone boson of a renormalizable Peccei-Quinn theory requires an anomaly coefficient $\gtrsim100$, implying a Landau pole for QCD. These tensions are relaxed for more strongly coupled dark gauge sectors.

CPU-ARC: Agentic Reasoning Caching for on-CPU Reasoning Reuse

Published: 2026-11-06 | Authors: Peter Belcak, Greg Heinrich, Saurav Muralidharan, Shizhe Diao, Pavlo Molchanov | Tags: cs.AI

Abstract

Large language models reinvent the wheel all the time. They conflate task-solving with the execution of the solutions, which forces them to solve every problem from scratch. We show that once a task has been reasoned through, the application of its solution to a new problem instance can be done very efficiently and with little involvement from language models. We introduce agentic reasoning caching, a method for constructing robust CPU-executable agentic reasoning caches (ARCs) from LLM reasoning traces. ARCs show on-par performance with frontier LLMs across a range of difficult mathematical and scientific tasks, are fully explainable, continually trainable, robust to input perturbations, and 16-291x cheaper to invoke than the source model.

Abstract

Deep learning training is difficult to verify within a scientific workflow: its architecture, hyperparameters, and procedure are rarely fixed until training has been attempted, and two honest runs never produce the same weights, so recomputation cannot serve as a check. We present SciCheck, a framework in which the scientist explores training configurations freely and posts a blueprint to a provenance ledger for each candidate they want verified, and a verifying scientist judges the resulting run in two layers: a statistical judge model that compares per-step gradient statistics against the verifier's own reference run, and a spot-check that re-executes randomly chosen training segments whose checkpoints are committed to the ledger one epoch at a time.
