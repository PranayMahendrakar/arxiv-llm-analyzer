Abstract

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) integrate information across modalities, but become unreliable when those modalities disagree. Under conflicting inputs, MLLMs may favor modalities that are not the most reliable for the task, thus motivating inference-time control of modality arbitration. We identify a small set of attention heads, termed arbitration heads, that causally influence whether predictions follow visual or textual evidence. Building on this, we introduce a lightweight, training-free intervention that biases these heads toward a designated trusted modality at inference time.
